= Coverage and Known Gaps
Ribose
v1.0, 2026-10-28

Interscript encodes authority transliteration standards as executable maps.
Every standard the project holds has been audited against the corpus; this
page states what is covered, what is held but not yet encoded, and what is
deliberately not done.

The authoritative reference-to-corpus inventory lives in the
https://github.com/interscript/maps/blob/main/docs/coverage/README.adoc[maps repository].

== What is covered

The corpus contains 300 authority-backed systems, including ALA-LC,
BGN/PCGN, ISO, ODNI (the Intelligence Community standard), UN/UNGEGN,
ICAO, DIN, BSI, and national systems. Each map carries its standard's own
example rows and is gated by ISC parse checks, embedded tests, and
cross-runtime parity.

The neural layer is separate from the map layer. A language can have a map
without a model; most of the corpus does. Shipped models currently cover
Hebrew, Khmer, Thai, Persian, Arabic, and Urdu.

== Held standards not yet encoded

Fifteen deterministic tables are held but not yet encoded. Each is a
complete table with its own example rows. None needs training data to
encode; a training corpus is required only to train a model on top of the
map.

=== ODNI / ICS 630-01

* **Kurdish** (Annex G) — the Intelligence Community table for Kurdish
  personal names: Perso-Arabic (Sorani) to Latin, using the 26 Latin
  letters plus the apostrophe, deliberately without diacritics. It is a
  different system from the BGN/PCGN Kurdish 2007 map.
* **Chinese** (Annex D) — not a Han-character romanizer. The standard
  applies to names already written in Hanyu Pinyin and normalises that
  rendering (surname title case, no umlauted u, spacing, apostrophes). Its
  appendices include conversion tables from Wade-Giles and Yale to pinyin.
  Han to pinyin is a separate problem that needs a character lexicon; the
  corpus already has the BGN/PCGN Hanyu Pinyin map for that.

=== DIN

* **DIN 31635** — Arabic, Ottoman Turkish, Persian, Kurdish, and Urdu.
* **DIN 31636** — Hebrew.
* **DIN 32706** — Armenian.
* **DIN 32708** — Japanese.
* **DIN 1460-2** — non-Slavic Cyrillic alphabets.

DIN 32707 (Georgian), DIN 33903 (Tamil), and DIN 33904 (Devanagari) are
already encoded.

=== BSI

* **BS 2979:1958** — transliteration of Cyrillic and Greek characters.
* **PD 6505:1982** — guide to the romanization of Korean.

=== BGN/PCGN

* **Burmese**, **Lao**, **Shan**, and **Inuktitut** — complete BGN/PCGN
  tables for scripts whose maps are not yet encoded.
* **Hebrew** and **Khmer** — complete BGN/PCGN tables; Hebrew is currently
  covered only by the ICAO 9303 map, and Khmer by a neural model, not by a
  BGN/PCGN rule map.

== Not doing: neural models for five languages

Burmese, Dzongkha, Inuktitut, Lao, and Shan have complete BGN/PCGN tables,
but no gold script-to-romanization data exists to train a neural model.
Interscript withholds a model rather than ship a bad-quality one. This
blocks training, not map coverage: the deterministic maps for these
languages remain in scope.

== Source documents not held

Some verified maps have no archived source document in the project:

* **ALA-LC** — one table document is archived of the 78 tables in the
  official catalogue. The 55 encoded ALA-LC maps are verified corpus
  entries, but their source tables are not held.
* **ISO** — ISO 9, ISO 9984, and ISO/TR 11941 are held; the other ISO
  standards represented in the corpus are not.
* **UN/UNGEGN** — the schedules are not held.

These are archival gaps, not encoding gaps.

== How the audit works

A system is only called missing after checking, in order: the corpus on
main, open pull requests, merged history, and maps under other authority
prefixes. The corpus is the authority for what is encoded; a diff of held
PDFs against `main` alone is not enough.
